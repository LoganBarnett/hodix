This document records where hodix is headed.  The hodocule facts exist, and so
do the secrets and root certificate modules, though no hodon runs them yet.
Nothing else here is built yet.  [[*First hodocule][First hodocule]] is the order it gets built in.

* Federation

The first goal after the facts baseline is to express a hodocule.

Any hodon can federate with another.  The owners of both hodons exchange a
handshake.  Each owner states which permissions and resources the other hodon is
granted.  Either party can end the grants or the connection itself at any time.

Hodon names are unique within a hodocule and nowhere wider.  A bridge resolves a
peer's names to one peer, so the federation facts reject a peer whose name
collides with home or with another peer.

** Federated and visited hodons

A hodon relates to another hodon in one of two ways.

A federated hodon is a member of the same hodocule.  The relationship is between
two hodons and both owners agree to it.  It lives in the facts of both.  Home
runs a WireGuard link to the peer, a bridge for the peer's names, and DNS that
sends the peer's names to that bridge.  Devices gain the peer's names in their
tunnel domain list and nothing else.  Only a device that roams onto the peer
trusts its root, and then only for the peer's subtree.

A visited hodon is a venue.  The relationship is between one device and the
venue, and home is not a party to it.  Nothing about the venue enters home's
facts or home's network.  The device installs a constrained anchor for the
venue's root and resolves the venue's names locally while standing there.  What
a venue offers guests, and on which network, is a fact of the venue.  Home
grants nothing and learns nothing.

Both relationships use the same device-side trust.  They differ in whether
home's network takes part.

** Connection

Two federated hodons connect to each other over WireGuard.  The link is peer to
peer across the Internet.  Some esoteric means of direct connection may also
carry it.  Seeing both hodons bring up this link is one of the key proofs we
want.

** Reaching a service on another hodon

A hodocule gains a hodon without any device trusting a new certificate.  A
device that roams onto the new hodon is the exception.  [[*Roaming][Roaming]] bounds what it
trusts.

Each hodon's root certificate covers every name under ~hodon.internal~.  It is
not limited to the hodon's own domain.  Each hodon also runs an nginx instance.
That instance translates TLS with its twin on the other hodon.

Take a client on ~proton~ that wants ~nextcloud.heartwood.hodon.internal~.  The
hodon ~heartwood~ is federated with ~proton~.

1. The client's DNS resolves the name to the nginx instance on ~proton~.
2. That nginx answers under the root certificate of ~proton~.  Every client
   device on ~proton~ already trusts it.
3. It re-encrypts the request under the TLS of ~heartwood~.  The request then
   crosses the WireGuard interface.
4. The nginx instance on ~heartwood~ forwards the request to the Nextcloud
   instance in ~heartwood~.

** Roaming

A device belongs to one hodon.  That hodon is its home.  Away from home, the
device reaches home's names and every federated peer's names through a split
tunnel to home.  The home bridge is the only middle box that ever sees the
device's credentials.  The namespace is shared, so home claims only what home
can answer.  Names of any other hodon resolve on the network the device is
standing on.

The price is a boomerang.  A device standing on a federated hodon reaches that
hodon's own services by way of home.  A later client module may route that
hodon's names locally while the device stands on it.  The DHCP domain is hint
enough.  Trust is TLS's job, so a false claim buys the claimant nothing but a
denial of service it already had.  Home's own names never leave the tunnel.

Trust is asymmetric.  A device trusts its home root for all of ~hodon.internal~.
It trusts any other hodon's root for that hodon's own subtree only.  The device
re-signs the foreign CA key under a name constraint and installs the result as
the anchor.  Validators do not check the signature on an anchor, so no other
party signs anything.  A foreign bridge then cannot present a home name to the
device, so home credentials never transit a foreign bridge.

A hodon with no relationship to home is reachable and untrusted.  Its names
resolve locally and its certificates fail validation until the device visits it.
Every hodon service speaks TLS so that failure is the whole story.

* First hodocule

The minimum viable hodocule is two hodons.  One is ~proton~.  The other is a
household's single machine, ~heartwood~ in the examples here.  Each runs
Nextcloud.  Users share folders across the pair, and a search for a word finds a
user's own photos and the photos shared with them, ranked by meaning.

** Budget

Resident memory is the budget.  The second hodon is one old machine that is, in
effect, a household's local Internet.  Disk is cheap and a few gigabytes more of
it is fine.  Every service below is measured on that machine before it lands and
after, and the number is recorded.

** Rules

1. The machine runs the household's network from the first day.  The router's
   DMZ points at it, and it serves DHCP and DNS.  NixOS has run ~proton~ this
   way for over a year, and a second copy is not where the risk is.
2. The link between the hodons is also the only management path to the second
   machine.  It is stood up with both machines on one network, before the second
   one travels.
3. Every service the machine runs already exists in ~proton~'s configuration.
   Each one is pulled out into a hodix module that reads the facts, and ~proton~
   then imports the hodix module in place of its own.  Nothing is written twice
   and nothing is left behind.
4. Only WireGuard listens on the Internet.  Every other service is reached on
   the LAN or over the link, Nextcloud included.  Sharing with another hodon
   never needs more than that.  Sharing with someone who has no hodon is a later
   decision and not a hole punched now.

** Backfill

What moves out of ~proton~'s configuration and into hodix.  Each item is a
module driven by the facts, with ~proton~ switched over to it as the proof.

1. [ ] Secrets.  agenix-rekey and the generators, driven by the facts.  A host's
       key is a fact and the repository layout is one setting.
2. [ ] The hodon's root certificate, and the certificates services present under
       it.
3. [ ] LDAP from the facts.  ~hodon.users~ and ~hodon.groups~ become the
       directory, with ~members~ already flattened.
4. [ ] LDAP authentication for services from the facts.  Nextcloud is the first
       consumer.
5. [ ] DHCP from the facts.  A lease per host interface, and the machine handed
       out as the DNS server.
6. [ ] DNS from the facts.  Every host name, aliases, blocking by profile, the
       public address kept current, and peer domains sent to the bridge.
7. [ ] WireGuard device access from the facts, so a device away from home
       reaches it.  This is not yet the roaming client module.
8. [ ] NFS provider and consumer from the facts, for a hodon whose storage and
       services sit on different machines.
9. [ ] Restic from the facts.  What each host backs up and where.  Cross hodon
       destinations come after.
10. [ ] Nextcloud from the facts.  LDAP authentication, the data location, and
        trusted domains.  Nextcloud keeps its own CA bundle for outgoing
        requests, and peer names arrive through the bridge under the hodon's
        root, so the module imports that root with occ's
        ~security:certificates:import~.

** Order

1. [ ] Services enter the card.  ~hodon.hodocule.peers.<name>.services~ names
       what a peer exports and where.  The bridge forwards exactly these.
       Nextcloud trusts exactly these.  ~proton~ adopts the hodocule module with
       no other facts, since ~hodon.name~ and the peer's card are enough.
2. [ ] The link.  WireGuard between the two hodons, derived from the facts.  A
       hodon with no endpoint dials the one that has one.  The deploy tool
       learns the second domain and the second flake.
3. [ ] The bridge.  The nginx pair from [[*Reaching a service on another hodon][Reaching a service on another hodon]].
       Home answers for the peer's domain under a wildcard certificate from its
       own root, verifies the peer's root upstream, and forwards only exported
       services.  Home's DNS sends the peer's names to the bridge.
4. [ ] The second hodon, on one machine.  Everything under [[*Backfill][Backfill]] except NFS,
       plus the bridge and the link.  Nextcloud on Postgres.
5. [ ] Nextcloud federation.  Trusted servers derive from exported services.
       Users then share folders across the pair.  No identity federation is
       needed, since a federated share addresses a user as user at server.
6. [ ] Photo search.  Sightline on each hodon, the app and its embedding
       service.  It embeds Nextcloud's own previews, so a federated share is
       indexed by the receiving hodon as it arrives.  The hodons never agree on
       a model.  Immich stays where it already runs and the second hodon runs
       none.  The first patch carried is a smaller text tower.  Sightline's
       default needs 2.2 GB at query time, and the ~ViT-B-32~ openai tower
       Immich publishes is about a tenth of that at the cost of multilingual
       queries.
7. [ ] The proof.  A user on one hodon shares a folder with a user on the other.
       The receiver searches one word and the result holds their own photos and
       the shared ones.

** Checks before building

1. Whether the second site has a public address or sits behind carrier NAT.
   This decides which hodon has the endpoint.
2. That Nextcloud generates previews for federated mounts.  Sightline indexes
   nothing else.
3. That the Nextcloud nixpkgs ships is one Sightline supports.
4. The second machine's memory, before anything is installed on it.

** After

1. People names from Immich into the XMP sidecars Immich already writes, and an
   indexer that turns sidecar keywords and people into Nextcloud system tags.
   Both are a second pass over existing photos and nothing in the MVP waits on
   them.
2. Grants, once identity federation is designed.
3. The hodons back each other up over the link.
4. The roaming client module, with the DHCP hint.
5. Monitoring from the facts.  DHCP, DNS, and WireGuard dashboards already exist
   in ~proton~'s configuration and follow the same backfill rule.
6. Guest access.  A person with no hodon is issued a one-off WireGuard profile
   and becomes a device of the hodon with narrow access.  This is the only way
   anything is ever shared outside the hodocule, and nothing new listens for it.
